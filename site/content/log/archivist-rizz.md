So I identify as asexual--demisexual if you want to be specific about
labels--but these days I'm a bit more shy about sharing that part of my identity
than I used to be.

Not out of shame or fear--I'm just tired of people making assumptions about
precisely what that means for me. This is somewhat difficult to avoid when you
run an archive of asexual and aromantic history; folks (understandably) tend to
assume those identities apply to me.

The most common misconception is that "asexual" means celibate or sex-averse.
And for many people it does! But that's not quite the case for me. I generally
need to know someone fairly well for that path to open up, and my relationship
with my body is a complicating factor, but it's not a "no, never" situation.

I don't like people making assumptions about me in general, but especially when
those assumptions lock me out of experiences. Well-meaning friends have withheld
invitations--or even discouraged me from attending events--because they have the
impression that I'm too asexual (or autistic!) to have a good time. I also get
anxious that it affects how people approach me personally.

I just want to feel desired, ya know?

Pretty much all my friends are in either furry or kink--two notably sex-positive
communities. And I think that's great! I enjoy being in sex-positive spaces,
regardless of how or to what extent I choose to engage with it. But being
surrounded by horny furries does exacerbate some of these feelings--feeling left
out, not feeling desired, feeling ẘ̨ͤͦ̊̎͝e͆ͥī̵̛̲r̹͓̀̿͟ḑ̎ about my body.
