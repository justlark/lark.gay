_This post was written for the
[September 2026 Midway Menagerie](https://energyscarf.net/blog/midway-menagerie-september-2026-call-for-submissions/),
where the theme is "identity and the self"._

"Othering"--treating or portraying a group of people as fundamentally different
from yourself--is an act of hate. The point is to malign groups you want gone.

It's also a concept I identify with, and I want to talk about that.

I use it/it pronouns, which is similar contradiction; to call someone an "it" is
to dehumanize them, often with hateful intent. And in that context it certainly
doesn't feel good. But among friends, being referred to with it/it pronouns
actually feels affirming in a gender-y way.

I use other objectifying language to refer to myself as well. "Thing" feels
pretty good. I like using third-person language in first- and second-person
contexts ("How's Lark doing today?"). Also using "lark" as a common noun ("It's
a lark!").

It is definitely not obvious to most people how objectifying language could be
affirming for me, and for that reason most people are hesitant to use it.

I don't really identify with concepts like therian or otherkin; I don't feel a
strong affinity toward a particular animal, object, or concept. It's moreso that
my gender goals feel misaligned with humanity in an abstract sense.

Staring HRT[^1] was a critical step in my transition, and one of the best
decisions I've ever made. I've managed to hit many of the goals I had set out
for myself when I started my transition, which feels good. The more time I've
spent living in my new body, however, the more I've come to realize there are
still aspects of my physiology that don't feel aligned with my gender. But of
the various alternatives, none of the options available to humans feel correct.

This is a really weird spot to be in, particularly because in some cases I don't
even understand what an ideal state _would_ look like. There's just a vague
sense of wanting some secret third option.

My affinity for objectifying language isn't just about my body; it represents a
more fundamental disconnect with how humans operate. This is definitely tied up
with my neurotype as well--spending much of my life studying human interactions
to try and crack the secret code of conventional social norms.

Reclaiming the concept of _other_ is a rejection of humanity, but also an
invitation to explore and own my full authentic self.

Relating all of this to this month's theme, I think part of what draws me to
furry is the dehumanizing nature of it. I've
[talked before](@/log/its-not-about-the-animals.md) about how my connection to
this community isn't really about being an animal person, but I do appreciate
feeling less alone sitting with these feelings. While not universal, wanting to
live in the body of your fursona is such a common experience here that I feel
solidarity with the community.

I don't really know what I am. I'm not quite an animal, but hanging out with the
animal people helps me feel okay being _other_.

[^1]:
    HRT, or hormone replacement therapy, is a course of medical treatment where
    you take synthetic hormones to supplement or replace certain hormones in
    your body, typically testosterone or the various estrogens. Both cisgender
    and transgender people take HRT for various reason.
